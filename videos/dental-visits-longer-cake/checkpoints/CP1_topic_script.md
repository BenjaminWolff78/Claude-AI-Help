## Script (17 sentences, 223 words, 292 syllables, average 13.1 words per sentence, 12% fragments)
A dentist made appointments longer, and his patients started bringing him cake.
You see, the whole town hated his chair so much, they kept their toothaches instead.
So Doctor Hal adds three slow, minty minutes to every visit, and across the street, Doctor Vance laughs so hard his mask falls off.
"Longer? Why not add a second drill?"
By Friday, the whole street calls Hal Doctor Slowpoke, and even his receptionist times him with an egg timer.
And his first patient, a truck driver named Pratt, still screams through the drilling, so loud the waiting room empties.
So how does more time in the chair make it hurt less?
Well, on his way out, Pratt books his next visit.
Then his whole bowling team books, and by spring, Vance's waiting room is just Vance.
That's the trick.
Your memory doesn't add up the pain, it keeps two snapshots, the worst moment and the last one.
Think about a movie with a great ending, you forget the boring middle.
In one study, people chose to put a hand back in painfully cold water for longer, just because it warmed up a little at the end.
So those minty three minutes become the whole memory.
Sneaky, or the nicest thing a dentist ever did?
Now Vance sits in Doctor Slowpoke's waiting room, holding a cake.

## Facts and sources
- **Cold-water study:** in a 1993 study, people held a hand in 14 °C water for 60 s in one trial. In the other trial they held it for 90 s, with the water warming by about 1 °C over the last 30 s. Most of them (22 of 32) chose to repeat the longer trial.
  Source: Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber & Redelmeier, "When More Pain Is Preferred to Less: Adding a Better End", *Psychological Science* 4(6), 1993.
  The script says "painfully cold water", not "ice water", because 14 °C is cold, not ice.
- **Colonoscopy trial (the real model for Hal's three minutes):** 682 patients took part. For half of them, the scope was left still for up to three extra, milder minutes at the end. Those patients rated the whole procedure as less unpleasant, and were somewhat more likely to come back.
  Source: Redelmeier, Katz & Kahneman, "Memories of colonoscopy: a randomized trial", *Pain* 104, 2003.
- **Peak-end rule:** memory weights the worst moment and the ending and mostly ignores how long something lasted. A 2022 meta-analysis supports this, but the ending is not the whole story.
  Source: Alaybek et al., *OBHDP* 2022.
- **Dramatized:** Hal, Vance, Pratt, the town, the cake and the egg timer are invented. Dentists polishing at the end of a visit is a story device, not a claimed study. The description will say so.
