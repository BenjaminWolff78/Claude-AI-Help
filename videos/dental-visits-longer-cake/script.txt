A dentist made appointments longer, and his patients started bringing him cake.
You see, the whole town hated his chair so much they kept their toothaches instead.
So Doctor Hal adds three slow minty minutes to every visit, and across the street Doctor Vance laughs so hard his mask falls off.
"Longer? Why not add a second drill?"
By Friday the whole street calls Hal Doctor Slowpoke, and even his receptionist times him with an egg timer.
And his first patient, a truck driver named Pratt, still screams through the drilling so loud the waiting room empties.
So how does more time in the chair make it hurt less?
Well, on his way out Pratt books his next visit.
Then his whole bowling team books, and by spring Vance's waiting room is just Vance.
That's the trick.
Your memory doesn't add up the pain, it keeps two snapshots, the worst moment and the last one.
Think of a movie with a great ending, you forget the boring middle.
In one study people chose to put a hand back in painfully cold water for longer, just because it warmed up at the end.
So those minty three minutes become the whole memory.
Sneaky, or the nicest thing a dentist ever did?
Now Vance sits in Doctor Slowpoke's waiting room, holding a cake.
