# He Made Dental Visits Longer and Got Cake

## Description
A dentist made every visit three minutes longer, and his patients started bringing him cake. Was that genius or just sneaky?

The mechanism is real. Memory rates an experience by its worst moment and its last one, and mostly ignores how long it lasted (the peak-end rule). In a 1993 study, most people chose to repeat a longer cold-water trial because it ended a little warmer. In a 2003 colonoscopy trial, patients given a gentler last few minutes remembered the whole procedure as less unpleasant.

The dentists, the town, the cake and the egg timer are made up for the story. The studies are real (Kahneman et al. 1993, Psychological Science; Redelmeier, Katz & Kahneman 2003, Pain).

Sneaky, or the nicest thing a dentist ever did?
