## Facts and sources
- Pincé (Sarthe, France), September 2012: the mayor bought 62 Loué laying hens; 31 households, a third of the village's families, took two each and signed a two-year commitment; the aim was to cut the waste collected and the incentive waste fee. (consoGlobe, "Réduire les déchets avec des poules"; AFP, 28 March 2012)
- Belgian towns (Mouscron, Antwerp, the province of Limburg) handed out hens; recipients signed an agreement not to eat them for at least two years. (Brussels Times, citing the BBC)
- A hen eats about 150 g of bio-waste a day. (Brussels Times; Colmar's "One family, one hen" figures)
- About a third of a household bin is organic waste (a French mayor's figure, Objectif Gard); in a Thouars test, hens cut bin volume by about a third (Reporterre).
- "Paid for its garbage by the ton" simplifies the incentive fee (households pay by the amount thrown away; towns pay per tonne for disposal). **Probably** accurate for Pincé's area; worded as a simplification.
- Dramatised: Bruno, Mayor Cluck, the honking, lifting bins with one finger, Bruno asking for hens. The hook's "banned" stands for the two-year no-eating pledge; the description says so.
