## 4. Final script (225 words, 18 sentences, average 12.5 words, 0 fragments of ≤ 4 words)
> A farmer covered his oranges in ice to stop them from freezing. You see, a killer freeze is hitting Florida, and while his neighbor Duane lights fires under his trees, Earl turns on the sprinklers. Duane laughs, "He's making popsicles!", and bets a crate the grove is dead by morning. Earl shrugs: "Ice keeps 'em warm." Wait, ice keeps oranges warm? By nine, the leaves are glazed, and Duane's farmhands are laughing at him. By midnight, every orange is a glass ball, and the town calls him Popsicle Earl. At three a.m., a branch snaps under the ice, and Duane hollers, "Told ya, popsicles!" It's so cold the scarecrow asks to come in. Duane's thermometer hits 24, and four hours below 28 turns oranges to ice. But Earl never turns the water off. That's the trick: when water freezes, it actually gives off heat. Every drop that freezes warms the orange a little, so while the water keeps coming, it stays at 32, above 28. Ever see a tub of water in a cellar? Same trick: the tub freezes, so the jam jars don't. At sunrise, the ice slides off, and Popsicle Earl's oranges are full of juice. Duane still says it doesn't count, because technically, Earl froze his first. But he paid the bet anyway, with a crate of his own oranges, frozen solid.
