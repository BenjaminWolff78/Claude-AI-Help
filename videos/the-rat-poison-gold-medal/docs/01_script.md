# The Rat Poison Gold Medal 🏅

**Script (default formula: paradox hook, then "You see," by 6 s; voice "The Pharaoh 3" on eleven_v4, one take, one whole-file atempo)**

A runner won Olympic gold after his coach kept feeding him rat poison.
You see, the 1904 marathon is 25 miles of dusty road in 90-degree heat, and the organizer wants to study thirst, so there's just one well.
Cars full of officials drive ahead, kicking dust in every runner's face. "Keep up, boys!"
The dust is so thick the runners could chew it.
One runner breathes in so much he nearly dies.
Another, a mailman from Cuba, gets hungry, eats rotten apples off a tree, and lies down for a nap.
A third gets chased a mile off course by a dog.
So how does anyone win this?
Thomas Hicks, who's begging his coach for water.
His coach says no, and mixes him something else: raw egg, brandy, and a pinch of rat poison.
An official yells, "You're poisoning him!"
The coach shrugs. "Just a pinch."
Then he gives him another one.
That's the trick: in 1904, a tiny dose of rat poison was an energy drink.
Ever had so much coffee your hands started shaking?
It's that, but one pinch too many and you're dead.
So Hicks staggers on, seeing things, and his team carries him over the finish line.
He wins gold, eight pounds lighter.
But he wasn't the first man across. The first guy took a car.

## Structure check (v10.5 formula)
- Hook (13 words, about 3.4 s): finished absurd act, concrete object (rat poison), absurd part last, no name/year/place.
- "You see," starts right after the hook (about 3.5 s) and carries the setting (1904, 25 miles, 90 degrees, one well on purpose).
- Problem and mocking line by 6-20 s: dust cars, "Keep up, boys!"; exaggeration joke: dust so thick you could chew it.
- Three escalating beats: dust victim, apple nap, dog chase.
- Spoken question (about 20 s): "So how does anyone win this?"
- Trick named at about 62%: "That's the trick: ... a tiny dose of rat poison was an energy drink." Everyday comparison: too much coffee.
- Payoff: Hicks carried over the line, gold, eight pounds lighter. Twist/callback on the last word: "The first guy took a car." (loops into the hook: "A runner won...").

## Facts checked
- 1904 St. Louis Olympic marathon: 24 mi 1,500 yd, about 90 °F at the 3 p.m. start; 32 starters, 14 finishers.
- Organizer James E. Sullivan allowed only one water source (a well at about 12 miles), citing research on "purposeful dehydration".
- Officials' cars drove ahead and kicked up dust; William Garcia was found collapsed with internal injuries from breathing it.
- Andarín Carvajal, a Cuban postman, ate rotten apples from an orchard, took a nap and still finished fourth.
- Len Taunyane was chased about a mile off course by a dog.
- Thomas Hicks was refused water and given strychnine (a rat poison, used then in small doses as a stimulant) mixed with egg white and brandy, more than once; he hallucinated, was carried over the line, won in 3:28:53 and lost 8 pounds.
- Fred Lorz crossed the line first after riding about 10 miles in a car, and was disqualified.
- Sources: Wikipedia "Athletics at the 1904 Summer Olympics – Men's marathon"; Smithsonian Magazine.
