## Script (present tense after the hook; about 232 words)
An engineer spent his whole career making cars easier to crush.
You see, it's the 1950s, and a safe car means a tank. Thick steel. Nothing bends.
Crash one into a wall and the car's fine. The guy inside isn't.
So this guy draws the opposite: a soft nose, a soft tail, and a steel cage in the middle.
His boss goes, "You want our cars to fold?"
The salesmen laugh, and the whole factory names it the Paper Car.
So why build a car that crumples?
In 1959, they finally smash it into a concrete wall.
The whole front folds like a soda can. Everybody laughs.
Then somebody opens the door. The dummy inside is just sitting there. Fine.
Ever caught a water balloon? Stiff hands, and it bursts. Pull your hands back as it lands, and it survives.
Same balloon. Same speed. You just gave it more time to stop.
That's the trick. The crumpling nose is your hands pulling back. It turns a sudden stop into a slow squeeze, so the people inside get pushed instead of punched.
Then they crash the tank car. Not a scratch on it. The dummy flies into the wheel so hard its head pops off.
Today, every car on the road is a Paper Car.
And people still look at a wreck and go, "They don't build them like they used to." Exactly.

## Facts (checked)
- Béla Barényi (1907-1997) joined Daimler-Benz in 1939 and ran its pre-development department until 1972. He conceived the safety body with crumple zones in 1937 and patented it as DE 854157 (dated 28 Feb 1952): a rigid passenger cell with deformable front and rear zones. Before him the common belief was that a safe car must be rigid. (Wikipedia "Béla Barényi"; Mercedes-Benz "75 years of protection for occupants", via automotiveworld.com)
- The 1959 W111 "Fintail" was the first production car body built on the patent. Systematic crash tests with dummies began in 1959, and the first proper vehicle crash test took place in autumn 1959 (Guntram Huber, quoted by Mercedes-Benz).
- Physics: for the same change of speed, a longer stopping time means a smaller force (impulse). Crumple zones lengthen the crash pulse while the cell stays rigid.
- Script simplifications: the boss's line, the "Paper Car" nickname, the laughing salesmen and the dummy's head are invented story beats in the channel's comic style. They are not historical quotes. The engineer and the company stay unnamed (the hook formula keeps the subject anonymous, no real person is drawn as a likeness, and there are no real brands). "In 1959, they finally smash it into a concrete wall" matches the first proper crash test in autumn 1959.
