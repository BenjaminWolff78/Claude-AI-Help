## Facts (checked) and what is dramatised
- A frozen pipe usually splits not at the ice but downstream, between the ice blockage and the closed tap, where there is little or no ice: the growing ice pushes the trapped water toward the shut faucet and the pressure climbs until the pipe fails. Sources: Multnomah County frozen-pipe guide (University of Illinois research), LiveScience "Why do freezing pipes burst?", HowStuffWorks.
- An open, dripping tap relieves that pressure. It doesn't stop the water freezing, but it stops the burst. Sources: HowStuffWorks "dripping faucet prevent frozen pipes", EDT Engineers "Why Frozen Pipes Rupture", WebMD.
- Pick the tap farthest from where the water enters the house. Sources: This Old House, HowStuffWorks.
- Water expands about 9% when it freezes (This Old House). The damage often shows only at the thaw, when the ice plug stops sealing the split (HowStuffWorks).
- **Dramatised:** the street, the people, the sign, and the "last fat drop" at 3 a.m. That drop is probably what happens (the ice pushing the trapped water out of the open tap), but no source describes that exact moment.
