He let his tap drip all night in a freeze, and every other pipe on his street burst. The forecast says minus twenty, so his neighbor in the red jacket shuts every tap tight. The old man just opens his kitchen tap to a slow drip, right into a bucket. The neighbor laughs over the fence, "You're paying to water a bucket!" By morning, the bucket's half full. By the second night, the whole street calls him Drippy. Wait, why pour water down the drain all night? Then, on the third night, his drip stops. The neighbor grins, "Haha, froze anyway, Drippy!" But at three in the morning, the frozen tap coughs out one last fat drop. When the thaw comes, the neighbor's kitchen wall bursts open and sprays water across the floor. You see, ice in a pipe grows and shoves the water ahead of it toward the tap. With the tap shut, that water has nowhere to go, so the pipe splits wherever it's weakest. It's like squeezing a ketchup bottle with the cap screwed on. His open tap let the squeeze out, and that last fat drop was the ice pushing through. Plumbers really tell you to leave the farthest tap dripping on the coldest nights. So now, whenever the forecast says minus twenty, the guy in the red jacket is the first one dripping. And the old man slams the Drippy sign right into his yard.
