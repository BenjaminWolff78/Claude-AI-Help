# Why He Wanted His Potatoes Stolen 🥔

## Topic test
A pharmacist put armed guards around a potato field so that people would steal from it, and it worked: France started eating potatoes.
- **Facts (Wikipedia, "Antoine-Augustin Parmentier"):**
  - France banned growing potatoes in 1748; people thought they caused leprosy and saw them as hog feed.
  - As a prisoner of war in Prussia he had to live on potatoes.
  - Louis XVI gave him poor ground near Neuilly.
  - He guarded his potato patch at Sablons with armed guards by day and withdrew them at night so people could steal the potatoes.
  - He gave the king and queen bouquets of potato blossoms.
  - French dishes still carry his name (hachis Parmentier, potage Parmentier).

## Script (238 words, about 64.5 s at 3.7 words/s)
A man put armed soldiers around a potato field just so people would steal it.
You see, in France back then, everybody was sure potatoes were pig food that gave you leprosy.
But this guy had survived a war prison eating nothing but potatoes.
He begged farmers to grow them. Nobody would.
One farmer took a whole sack and fed it to his pig.
The whole town laughed. "Hey, it's the Pig Doctor!"
So he asked the king for a field and a few soldiers.
Soldiers? To guard pig food?
He planted every row and told the guards, "Guard them like gold."
The first night, nothing. The second night, nothing.
The soldiers started taking bets on how dumb this was.
Even they called him the Pig Doctor.
The third night, they came back at sunrise, and the whole field was picked clean.
That's the trick.
Nobody wants pig food. But if the king's soldiers guard it, it must be worth stealing.
And every night at sundown, he quietly sent the guards to bed.
Ever wanted to open a door just because it said "Staff only"? Same thing.
Stolen potatoes ended up in a hundred gardens.
Then he gave the king a potato flower, and the king wore it on his coat.
Suddenly every noble wanted one.
Today French menus still name potato dishes after him.
Not bad for the Pig Doctor.
The pigs, though, never got another potato.

## Craft checks
- **Hook and "You see,":** "You see," at about 3.2 s, with the setting folded in.
- **Doubters, mockery and nickname:** doubters by 6 s ("everybody was sure"). The mockery escalates: one farmer, then the whole town (about 17 s), then even the soldiers. The nickname "Pig Doctor" returns at the payoff.
- **Question and fake-out:** the spoken question "Soldiers? To guard pig food?" comes at about 23 s. The fake-out ("first night, nothing; second night, nothing"; the soldiers bet against him) is at about 43-46%.
- **Rule of three:** three nights; the third breaks the pattern.
- **Trick and comparison:** "That's the trick." at 60% (about 38.5 s). The everyday comparison is the "Staff only" door.
- **Planted detail:** the farmer's pig eats the first sack, and at the end the pigs never get another potato.
- **Hero and quotes:** the hero only speaks in one-liners ("Guard them like gold."). Quoted lines are under 10 words, and no sentence is over 25 words.
- **Light arc:** warm market and field by day, a deep night blue for the three nights and the trick, then bright palace gold for the payoff.
