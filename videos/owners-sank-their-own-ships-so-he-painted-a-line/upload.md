**Title:** Owners Sank Their Own Ships, So He Painted a Line 🚢

**Description:**
Victorian ship owners overloaded old, over-insured ships — and a ship that sank paid out more than it was worth. Then MP Samuel Plimsoll fought for one painted line on every hull. Parliament dropped his bill, he shook his fist at the Speaker, and the public took his side. Today every cargo ship on Earth carries his circle. 🚢

Sources: History Hit, "The Plimsoll Line: How Samuel Plimsoll Made Sailing Safer"; The Victorian Web, "Samuel Plimsoll" (the 1875 outburst, the 1876 Act, owners painting the line where they liked — even on the funnel — and the 1890 rule).
Dramatised for the story: the owner and his "She'll float", the nickname "Paintbrush Plimsoll", the paint pot and the toy boat in the barrel.
