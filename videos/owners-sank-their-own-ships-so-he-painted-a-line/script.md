# Owners Sank Their Own Ships, So He Painted a Line 🚢

**Topic test:** in the 1870s British ship owners over-insured rotten, overloaded "coffin ships", so a ship was worth more sunk than afloat. MP Samuel Plimsoll fought for a compulsory load line. Parliament dropped his bill in 1875; he called members "villains" and shook his fist in the Speaker's face, then apologised. The 1876 Merchant Shipping Act made the line compulsory, but owners chose where to paint it (some painted it on the funnel) until the position was fixed in 1890. Lane: incentives that backfired + "everyone said it would fail, then it won". **Reusable rule:** a loaded ship sits deeper; below a set depth every wave comes over the deck, so the safe limit is a mark on the hull, not the owner's word.

## Script (243 words)
Ship owners loaded their own ships until they sank, and got rich doing it.
You see, in Victorian Britain, an old ship insured for more than it was worth was worth more sunk.
So owners piled on cargo until the deck was almost underwater.
The owner pats the hull. "She'll float."
She doesn't.
Insurance pays out, and he buys two more.
Over two thousand sailors went to court for refusing to climb aboard.
Then a politician named Samuel Plimsoll shows up with a paint pot.
His plan to save every sailor? One line of paint.
The owners howl. "Paintbrush Plimsoll!"
In 1875, Parliament drops his law completely.
So Plimsoll marches up to the Speaker, shakes his fist in his face, and calls them all villains.
He has to apologize, but the newspapers love it, and Britain takes his side.
A year later the law passes. Every ship needs a line.
One catch: owners paint it wherever they like. One paints it on the smokestack.
Here's the trick. Load a ship heavier and it sinks deeper, until every wave walks onto the deck.
Picture a toy boat in a barrel: one more penny, and the water's over the line.
In 1890, the government finally decides where the line goes.
So the next time that owner pats his hull, the water's over the line, and his ship stays home.
Today every cargo ship on Earth carries Paintbrush Plimsoll's circle.
Now when an owner says "she'll float," she has to.
