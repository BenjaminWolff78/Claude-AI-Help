## Script (242 words; voice 63.8 s; "You see," at 3.2 s)
To stop a fire, they blew up the houses it hadn't reached yet.
You see, it's 1666, London's made of wood, and a bakery just caught fire.
The mayor takes one look, goes, "One bucket could handle that," and goes back to bed.
By morning, the wind throws it roof to roof. The bakery. The street. The whole riverfront.
Buckets do nothing. The cathedral's lead roof melts and runs down the street like soup.
So a soldier rolls in barrels of gunpowder.
The street goes, "You're blowing up our houses?!"
He just goes, "No house, no fire."
They call him Captain Kaboom.
First they hook down a few houses, and the fire jumps the gap like it's nothing.
"See? Pointless."
So how do you stop a fire that can jump a street?
Ever set up dominoes? Tip one, the whole line goes. Pull three out of the middle, and it stops.
Same dominoes. Same push. Nothing left to knock over.
That's the trick. Fire can only eat what it can reach. Make the gap too wide, and it starves.
On day three, Captain Kaboom blows a gap a whole street wide. Boom. Boom. Boom.
The wind drops, the fire roars up to the gap, and finds nothing to eat.
And the mayor finally gets to use his one bucket.
Today, wildfire crews still burn the trees ahead of a fire on purpose.
And people still say you can't fight fire with fire.

## Facts checked
- **The fire:** it started in a bakery on Pudding Lane in the early hours of 2 September 1666. Most of London was timber, and pitch and tar were stored along the riverside wharves.
- **The mayor:** Lord Mayor Bloodworth dismissed the fire with a crude remark (here softened to "One bucket could handle that"). He refused to pull houses down because the owners could not be found, and he later went home to bed.
- **Buckets, hooks and failed gaps:** leather buckets and firehooks were the standard kit. Demolition by hand came too late and too slow, and the fire jumped gaps, even crossing the River Fleet, on the strong east wind.
- **The lead roof:** St Paul's lead roof melted on the third night. John Evelyn wrote of "the melting lead running down the streets in a stream".
- **Gunpowder:** on Tuesday 4 September (day three), the Tower of London garrison blew up houses with gunpowder to make wide firebreaks.
- **The stop:** the wind dropped that evening, and the gunpowder breaks held on 5 September. About 13,200 houses were destroyed.
- **Today:** wildfire crews burn out the fuel ahead of a fire ("backburning" or "burning out") to create a firebreak.

**Dramatised (comic story beats, not history):** the single soldier and his nickname "Captain Kaboom"; the homeowner's line; "See? Pointless."; the mayor's bucket at the end; the soup gag staging. No real person is drawn as a likeness. The mayor, the soldiers and the city stay generic, and there are no real brands.
