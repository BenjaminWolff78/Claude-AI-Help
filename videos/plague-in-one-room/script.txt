They put a deadly plague in one room of a video game, and it escaped. You see, the boss in that room casts a blood curse that jumps from player to player. It's meant to stay in that room and fade in ten seconds. But hunters fight with pets, and a cursed pet gets tucked away mid-fight so it can't die. Nobody wrote the line that cures a tucked-away pet. Back in the capital, Gary calls his wolf out in the market. His buddy laughs: "Relax, it's a ten-second curse." Then the buddy drops dead. The curse kills the strongest hero in thirty seconds. Then the crowd drops, then the whole market. Wait, how does a ten-second curse take a whole city? Because the shopkeepers catch it too, and they can't die, so they stand there, sick, selling bread. Healers rush in and keep the sick alive long enough to infect everyone. Some flee to the woods. New players stand at the gates warning everyone off. And a few catch it on purpose to run into crowds. The company tries quarantine zones, which players walk around, and server restarts, which bring it right back. After a week, they rewind the entire world and make every pet immune. Two years later, real scientists wrote it up in medical journals, because real people had just shown how humans act in a pandemic. All because nobody cured one sick pet.
