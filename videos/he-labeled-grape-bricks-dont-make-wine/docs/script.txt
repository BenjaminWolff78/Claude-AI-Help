A guy beat Prohibition by selling grape bricks with a warning not to make wine.
You see, America just banned alcohol, and his grapes are wine grapes.
His neighbors rip out their vines. One laughs, "Nobody's buying a grape brick, Brick Boy."
He just smiles. "Read the label."
Every brick gets a warning: do not dissolve this in a gallon of water, and do not leave the jug in a cupboard for twenty-one days.
The whole town laughs. Who puts a warning on juice?
Then federal agents pull up. The neighbors line the fence to watch Brick Boy get arrested.
An agent picks up a brick, reads the warning out loud... and puts it back. Because Washington just ruled it legal.
Bricks fly off the shelves. They even send a guy to your house to set up the jug, then come back and bottle it.
That's the trick. The law banned selling wine, but every family could make two hundred gallons of fruit juice at home a year. And if your juice happens to sit in a cupboard for twenty-one days, well, that's your juice.
It's like selling cake mix with a warning: do not bake at three-fifty.
California's vineyards almost double. The neighbors are planting their vines back.
And the government's top Prohibition lawyer quits... to become the grape farmers' lawyer.
So was Brick Boy a criminal, or just the only guy who read the law?
Twenty-one days later, the whole country is drinking juice. And it all started...
