## What the references show
**Focus**
- **No focus pulls inside a held shot**, in any of the 20 videos (depth report 1.5).
- **Focus goes to an object** in one of three ways:
  1. **An insert of the object.** About 22% of shots: G (dragon mouth, pendulum), T (the coin and caliper on the rail), the Fastest Draw sheet (bullet, holster, gun hand, phone).
  2. **A camera move that travels to the object.** W: the camera sits on the paper, then pushes into the two-shot. AE3: through the mirror.
  3. **The title object shown big in the payoff** (Y).
- **Blur is a per-beat choice:**
  - Inserts, close-ups and medium shots: 12-14 px.
  - Full shots: about 8 px.
  - Wides: about 5 px.
  - Establishing shots: almost sharp.

**Motion**
- The acting character moves the whole body every frame. X and T: sawing rocks the body, and the face changes on almost every frame.
- Groups react together on the word (AD1).
- The world never freezes:
  - T: the train light grows behind the worker.
  - AE2: smoke rises.
  - W: the soup pot swings into the foreground.
- **AC and AD say the opposite for bystanders:** in the references, people not acting stay completely still.
  - Your request ("make sure all of the objects/characters are in motion") overrides that.
  - So bystanders get small, purposeful moves. They never get looping sways or bobs, which AD also forbids and you dislike.

## What changes
1. **Characters: a life layer.** Every character on screen gets:
   - a glance, a 3-5 degree head tilt or a 1.5-2.5 degree weight shift at irregular 0.7-1.3 s intervals,
   - each eased over 10 frames and then held,
   - with no loops,
   - paused around scripted gestures, so those stay clean.
2. **Characters: real tasks for long holds.**
   - Brick Boy keeps cranking the press in the background of the wide.
   - Hank tugs in pulses. Two neighbours rip out vines behind him (the line says "neighbors", plural).
   - The kid leans in at the fizz and follows Mum to the pantry.
   - The crowd snickers and Hank points on "arrested".
   - Brick Boy restacks bricks while the agent walks up.
   - Mum watches the bricks fly off and claps, nods at "set up the jug", and smells the bottle.
   - The kid cheers, leans in and points at the bottle.
   - Hank pats the soil and wipes his brow.
   - Brick Boy tips his hat to the lawyer, shrugs at "criminal?" and taps the label on "read the law".
   - The lawyer blows on her stamp.
3. **Objects.**
   - Vine leaves ripple in the breeze, and their ink outlines move with them.
   - The bunting flutters.
   - The parked sedan idles, with a body shake and exhaust puffs.
   - In the kitchen:
     - The pot steams and the stove glow flickers.
     - The hanging lamp drifts and the curtains stir.
     - A pendulum wall clock swings.
     - The calendar page lifts.
     - The pantry jugs and the counter jug bubble (the juice fermenting).
   - In Washington, papers flutter under the fan and the flag ripples.
4. **Focus, reference-style.**
   - Focus sits on the subject's eyes or on the story object.
   - It changes only while the camera is already moving (the existing glides and pushes) or at a cut.
   - Blur levels follow the reference numbers above.
   - The object beats become true inserts with 14 px of background blur:
     - the label,
     - the jug,
     - the ruling,
     - the shelf,
     - the cake-mix box,
     - Hank's vine,
     - the payoff brick,
     - the glowing jug.
   - The NO-wine and 200 GALLONS cards move to just in front of the focus plane, so they stay sharp.
   - No camera moves, cuts or timings change, so the v10.9 numbers stay as they are.
