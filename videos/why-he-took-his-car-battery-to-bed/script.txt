He tucked his car battery into bed every night, and his was the only engine in camp that started. Every night at the logging camp, the new guy lugs all forty pounds of it inside. The foreman laughs, "Gonna read it a bedtime story too?" Night one, it sleeps by the stove. Night two, it gets a blanket. Night three, it gets his pillow, and he sleeps on the floor. By breakfast, the whole camp calls him the battery babysitter. Why? He won't say. The first cold morning, every truck starts just fine, and the foreman honks at him all the way to work. Then one night, it hits forty below. At six, the whole crew turns their keys, and the lot goes click, click, click. Even the foreman's giant truck just coughs and dies. Then the babysitter walks out with his battery steaming in his arms, hooks it up, and turns the key. First try. You see, a battery makes power with a chemical reaction, and the cold slows it down like honey from the fridge. At forty below, it loses more than half its power, just when a frozen engine needs twice as much. So the foreman shuffles over, "Hey, babysitter, got cables?" By lunch, the babysitter has jump-started the whole camp. And that night, the foreman tucks his own battery into bed.
