# The Skull That Was Worth More in Pieces 💀

## Description
A fossil hunter in Java paid farmers by the piece for ancient human bones, so they did the obvious thing: they broke every skull before they brought it in. Genius loophole, or the most expensive vandalism in science?

Based on a real story from 1930s Java. Ralph von Koenigswald paid local helpers for each piece of hominid fossil they found, and some specimens arrived smashed into splinters, because a skull in twelve pieces paid twelve times as much. Bill Bryson tells it as ten cents a piece in *A Short History of Nearly Everything*.

Agus, Joko, Sri and the village are invented for the story, and so are the counts (12 pieces, 3,000 pieces, 100 coins), the river excuse, the papaya and the tree-sap ending.

Genius or vandal?
