A farmer smashed a million-year-old skull with a hammer and got paid twelve times more. You see, a scientist named Ralph pays ten cents for every piece of ancient bone farmers find. So when Agus digs up a skull, his neighbor Joko laughs, "One piece, pal, ten cents." Agus smashes it into twelve pieces as the whole line laughs, and leaves with twelve coins. By Friday three thousand pieces fill Ralph's shed, because the whole village is swinging hammers. But one night, Ralph holds two pieces up to his lamp, and they fit perfectly. Joko grins, "You're done, Mr. Hammer." Ralph decides the river crushed it and pays another hundred coins. By the end of the month, kids smash bones before breakfast, ten cents a pop. Some call it vandalism, Agus calls it a raise. That's the trick: Ralph isn't paying for skulls, he's paying for pieces. And whatever you pay for, people make more of. Ever cut a papaya into twelve slices so it looks like more? It's still one papaya. By the time Ralph notices, his ancient human is a jigsaw puzzle with no picture on the box. So he changes the rule and only pays for whole skulls. Next morning, Mr. Hammer slaps down a skull glued together with tree sap: "One piece, pal."
