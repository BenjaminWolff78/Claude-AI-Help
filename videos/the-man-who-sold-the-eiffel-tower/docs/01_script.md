# The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower 🗼

**Script (231 words, about 62 s at the house pace, voice "The Pharaoh 3" on eleven_v4, atempo 1.28)**

This man is selling the Eiffel Tower, and the buyer is about to hand him a suitcase of cash.
It's Paris, 1925, and the newspapers say the tower is rusting and costs a fortune to paint.
You see, it was built for a world's fair, and it was only meant to stand for twenty years.
So con man Victor Lustig prints fake government letters and invites the city's biggest scrap dealers to a fancy hotel.
But how do you sell something you don't own?
First, he tells them it's top secret: seven thousand tons of iron, going to the highest bidder.
Then he rents a fancy car, drives them to the tower, and lets them climb it like he owns the place.
One dealer, André Poisson, wants it badly, but his wife isn't sure. "Why would a minister meet us in a hotel?"
So Lustig leans in and whispers: he's a poor official, and he'd like a little bribe.
That's the trick! In Paris, real officials took bribes, so asking for one made him look real.
It's like a salesman who admits his car has a scratch. Suddenly, you believe everything else.
Poisson pays the bribe and the price. Within an hour, Lustig is on a train to Vienna, counting cash.
And Poisson is too embarrassed to tell the police.
So a month later, Lustig comes back to Paris, and guess what he's selling.
