**Counts:**
- 2 hard cuts (limit 4).
- 4 dissolves of 8 frames: canvas to canvas three times with nobody in frame on either side, plus the photo-to-face subject-position match (board AE).
- 4 light dips of about 10 frames each: flashbulb x3 and oven glow x1. In a dip the frame floods with the scene's light and the cut happens under it. They replace every crossfade that had people on both sides (v10.10 rule 7).
- 15 camera relocations, each an eased glide of 22-42 frames.
- The long scene in the first 20 s is scene 3 (10.5 s, home base A). Scenes 8 and 10 are also long (8.6 s and 6.0 s).

## The title object changes on screen (v10.10 rule 3)
- **0.0-2.0 s:** the proof painting's last strokes go on in front of the court.
- **3.2-8.5 s:** the masterpiece is lifted out of the crate (large in frame), then turned round (7.9-8.5 s) to show the SOLD BY slip; the camera pushes onto the seller's photo.
- **27.0-28.9 s:** the masterpiece builds stroke by stroke: sketch, ground, cloak, face, ruff, hat, glass, highlights.
- **The trick, 43.9-49.3 s:**
  - 43.9-44.7 s: into the oven.
  - 44.7-45.3 s: the glow.
  - 46.0-46.7 s: tipped over the pipe while the cracks spread.
  - 47.1-47.9 s: ink rubbed into the cracks.
  - 48.1-49.3 s: the varnish yellows to 300 years.
- **49.9 s:** the aged painting matches into the courtroom's evidence painting.
- **54.8-60.8 s:** at the payoff the painting stands beside him on the left of the wide.
