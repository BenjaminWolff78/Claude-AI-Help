## Script (235 words, about 62 s)

A guy saved a bunch of lions by painting eyes on cows' butts.
You see, in Botswana, lions kept eating the cows, so the farmers kept shooting the lions.
Then one evening, he sees an antelope spot a sneaking lion, and the lion just quits.
So he grabs a paintbrush and paints giant eyes on the butt of every third cow.
"He's giving the cows makeup!"
One farmer laughs, then the whole fence laughs, and by lunch he's Butt Picasso.
Even the cows look embarrassed.
But why would a lion care about a butt?
Every night, he counts the herd. Sixty-two.
Then one morning, sixty-one. ← fake-out (43%)
The farmers fall off the fence laughing. "Nice work, Picasso!"
He just walks over and checks. The missing cow had no eyes.
"Trust the eyes."
The next night, another plain cow disappears. The farmers stop laughing.
Then a lion creeps up on a painted cow, sees two huge eyes staring back, and slowly backs away.
That's the trick.
A lion is an ambush hunter, and it only attacks something that isn't looking.
The second it thinks it's been spotted, the hunt is over.
Ever try to sneak a cookie while your mom's staring right at you? Same thing.
Four years and two thousand cows later, the painted ones lost to lions? Zero. The plain ones? Fifteen.
And Butt Picasso? Now the whole village is lining up for a paint job.

**How the script covers v10.8:**
- **Hook:** 12 words, then "You see," as sentence 2.
- **Spoken question:** "But why would a lion care about a butt?"
- **Mockery escalates:** one farmer, then the whole fence, then the whole village.
- **Nickname:** "Butt Picasso", which returns at the payoff.
- **Cryptic one-liner:** "Trust the eyes."
- **Planted detail:** the tally board reads 62, then 61; the payoff numbers are 0 vs 15.
- **Rule of three:** a plain cow goes, another plain cow goes, then the lion backs away from a painted cow.
- **Everyday comparison:** the cookie jar.
- **Exaggeration joke:** the embarrassed cows.
- **Loop:** "a paint job" runs into "A guy saved a bunch of lions...".
