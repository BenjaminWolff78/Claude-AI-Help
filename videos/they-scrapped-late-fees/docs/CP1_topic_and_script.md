## Script (229 words, 299 syllables; at 4.7 syllables per second about 63.6 s)
> A library ditched late fees, and got its books back by the truckload. You see, owe ten bucks here and Gary at the front desk blocks your card, so people just stop coming. So Marge, the head librarian, tells the board, "Let's forgive all of it." Gary laughs so hard he spills coffee on his coaster, a library book due back in 1994. "They'll keep everything!" By lunch the whole board calls her Free-For-All Marge. Marge just unlocks the book drop and says, "Leave it open." But why give up the money? The first week the box stays empty, and Gary tapes on a sign: "TOLD YOU." Then a kid slides in a comic, and a grandma wheels in cookbooks. Then some guy backs a pickup truck up the library steps. Three weeks later returns are up two hundred forty percent, and Free-For-All Marge is emptying the drop box with a shovel. That's the trick. The fine never brought books back; the shame of facing it kept people away. Ever dodged a friend you owe twenty bucks? The longer you wait, the harder you dodge. Sure, late people got off free, and on-time people got nothing. But next Monday, Gary sneaks up to the drop box with a coffee-stained book from 1994. Turns out the fastest way to get your books back is to stop asking for the money.

## Facts and sources (dramatized parts marked)
- Chicago Public Library went fine-free on 1 October 2019 and erased outstanding fines; within three weeks returns rose 240%. Sources: AP via Washington Times (3 Nov 2019); NBC Chicago; NPR/KPBS "We Wanted Our Patrons Back" (30 Nov 2019).
- About 30% of people on Chicago's South Side could not check out materials because they had hit the $10 fine limit (the card was blocked). Source: NPR/KPBS citing CPL's internal analysis.
- In some cities collecting fines cost more than it brought in (Boston and San Diego). Source: Nonprofit Quarterly. The script does not use this line; it was cut for length.
- The idea that fear or shame of facing the fine keeps books and people away: library officials quoted by Fox News / AP ("elimination of fear most book users have about coming face to face with their fines"). Colorado Virtual Library lists shame at facing staff as one reason fine-free libraries get more back.
- **Dramatized:** Marge, Gary, the board meeting, the "TOLD YOU" sign, the empty first week, the kid, the grandma, the pickup truck, the shovel and the 1994 book are invented. In the real story the decision came from the city, not one librarian. The hook and the numbers (ten dollars blocks the card; returns up 240% in three weeks) are the checked facts.
