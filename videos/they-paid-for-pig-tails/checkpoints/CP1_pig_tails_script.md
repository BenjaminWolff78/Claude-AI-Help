## Facts and sources
- Fort Benning, Georgia (a US Army base) offered a bounty on wild pigs from June 2007; pig density rose 23-130% and juveniles per adult female rose 191-219% during the program (Ditchkoff, Holtfreter and Williams 2017, Wildlife Society Bulletin 41(3):548-555, abstract on ADS; full paper on the Auburn deer lab site).
- $40 for every pig tail brought in (AP via NBC News, 2008).
- The authors' reason: hunters went for return on effort and "trophy" animals rather than the part of the population that drives growth. The script says only that (hunters shot the big boars, skipped the mothers). It does not claim bait fed the pigs.
- "A boar never has babies, the mothers do": basic biology. "More piglets than ever": the study's juveniles per adult female rose 191-219%.
- Dramatised: the shed, the people, the newspaper, the wanted poster, the chalk family tree, the fifty-dollar raise.
